# Strafe

A browser prototype that mixes Mirror's Edge parkour with CS surf and bhop movement.

- `dist/index.html` is the built, self-contained game (Three.js r128 from cdnjs).
- `src/physics.js` is the movement core: Source `CGameMovement` ground friction, accelerate,
  air accelerate with the 30 u/s air cap, `TryPlayerMove` clip-plane sliding, step moves, and
  Quake 3 style box-vs-brush sweeps with bevel planes. 100 tick fixed timestep, interpolated rendering.
  The Mirror's Edge layer (slide, wallrun, wall climb, climb kick with 180° turn, mantle, vault) sits on top.
- `src/map.js` builds a nine-station tutorial (one mechanic per station, with hint cards and a speed gate for air strafing) and ten levels: Surf (ten ramps over water round three corners), Bhop (block lines, islands and speed lanes over lava), Rooftops (a long beginner parkour run that turns three corners), Canyon (eleven alternating gorge banks), Stairway (climbing and falling block stairs round four corners), Crossover (slide, surf, bhop, wallrun and mantle, twice), and four long advanced levels: Downtown (a city parkour run through an office and along a crane), Cascade (nine extra-narrow, steep ramps with long side-switching jumps, the line bending three times between ramps, two climbs into the widest gaps, and checkpoints that drop you in from high ledges), Spire (small-block bhop with island-free corners) and Freerun (a full rooftop parkour run). It also builds the Gauntlet, a separate numbered set of six mixed levels (Lift, Drift, Sidewinder, Undertow, Freefall, Terminal), each harder than the last, made by `gauntlet()` from pads, bhop block lines and surf ramps: blocks shrink, ramps steepen, transfers widen and checkpoints thin out as the set goes on. Rooftops and Downtown are laid out with `cityKit`, which places pieces in per-leg frames so a run can turn corners and fills the skyline around the route. Surf, Bhop, Canyon, Stairway and Crossover are long turning routes built by `routeKit` from pads, corner pads, speed lanes, bhop block lines, surf ramps, gorge banks, slide roofs and wallrun billboards. The Night City is a third set of six mixed levels (Neon Mile, Afterhours, Overpass, Blackout, Signal, Skyline), also built by `routeKit`, that adds curved surf ramps: a `curve` piece bends a quarter turn on a radius, built from short straight pieces that run 40 units into each other so the joins sit under the face instead of catching the rider. Two joined curves make a hairpin. Picking a Night City level switches the world to night: a dark sky with stars, moonlight, lit office windows and neon trim on every pad and ramp edge. The Harbor is a fourth set of six levels (Dockside, Gantry, Breakwater, Tideline, Lighthouse, Riptide; keys H1 to H6), also built by `routeKit`, and the hardest in the game: mostly the narrowest (65°) ramps, transfers of 440 to 560 units across gaps of 1000 to 1150, the smallest blocks with the widest gaps, steep breakwater banks with long gaps, hairpins, and one checkpoint on Breakwater, Lighthouse and Riptide. Gantry plays easier than Dockside, so `harborOrder` puts it first (H1). With aim noise the bots clear them far less often than the Night City (`node test/difficulty.js`). Picking a Harbor level switches the world to dusk (`setTheme('dusk')` in game.js: a low orange sun, a purple sky with sunlit clouds, long shadows and the first windows lit), and `harborDetails()` in map.js dresses the route: railings, kerbs and hazard bollards on every pad, kerbs under the blocks, lamps on every ramp ridge, a gantry crane with a hanging container beside each leg's first big pad, and a lighthouse on Dockside and Lighthouse. The dressing is `deco`: drawn but never collided with, and not counted for falls.
- `src/audio.js` synthesizes all sound with Web Audio: footsteps, jumps, landings, wind/surf/slide loops tied to speed, and a generative score whose filter opens up with speed.
- `src/game.js` is rendering, input (pointer lock, CS sensitivity scale), HUD, timers and the menus: a title screen (Story, Play, Tutorial, Settings, Controls; it doubles as the pause menu) over a flythrough that cycles the levels, and a level select screen behind Play. Speedrun tools: P toggles practice mode (also Practice mode in the pause menu and a Practice button in level select), where Z saves the full movement state and run clock and X, F or a fall loads it, G and B jump to the next or previous checkpoint (wrapping to the start) and save there, and checkpoints and the finish show segment times (from your last load, checkpoint jump or checkpoint) against your best for that stretch this session; practice runs never touch best times, splits, ghosts, the leaderboard or stars. On a phone, Save, Load and CP+ buttons show while practicing; on a controller, Y saves, X loads and the D-pad skips checkpoints; the key display shows mouse turn direction next to the keys; and a strafe meter under the speed shows how much of the possible air-strafe gain each tick is getting (input only, so walls and ramps don't skew it), with the per-jump average beside the sync %.

Play: open `dist/index.html` in a browser (it loads Three.js from cdnjs, so it needs a connection). No install step; Node is only needed to build and run the tests.

Build: `node build.js` writes `dist/index.html`.

Desktop: `desktop/` wraps the same game in Electron for Windows, macOS and Linux. `cd desktop && npm install && npm start` runs it; `npm run dist` builds the downloads into `desktop/release/`. `desktop/prepare.js` builds the page with Three.js and the fonts embedded, so the app runs offline. Best times, settings and ghosts are kept in the app's local storage; the online leaderboard only exists in the claude.ai version, so it stays hidden. Pushing a `v*` tag runs `.github/workflows/desktop.yml`, which builds all three platforms and attaches them to a GitHub release.
Tests: `node test/sim.js` (movement checks),
`node test/w2w.js` (bot chains the rooftop wall-to-wall section), `node test/routes.js [level] [checkpoint]` (one bot finishes Surf, Bhop, Canyon, Stairway, Crossover, the six Night City levels and the six Harbor levels from the start and every checkpoint, riding curved ramps piece by piece), `node test/tutorial.js` (bot clears every tutorial station), `node test/advanced.js [level] [checkpoint]` (bots finish Cascade, Spire and Freerun; the Cascade bot plans each jump by trying several aim points in a cloned player). `node test/city.js [level] [checkpoint]` (bots finish Rooftops and Downtown). `node test/gauntlet.js [level] [checkpoint]` (one plan-reading bot finishes all six Gauntlet levels). Difficulty checks: `node test/hoplines.js [levels]` measures every bhop block line, holding a steady speed from 300 to 600 u/s and reporting what share of those speeds still lands each block (the line's tolerance for carrying too much speed). `NOISE=0.12 SEED=1` adds a random wobble to the aim of every bot (`test/noise.js`), and `node test/difficulty.js [levels] [seeds] [noise]` runs the noisy bots over each section and reports how often each level is cleared.

Difficulty: a bhop line gets easier to overshoot as blocks shrink and their spacing grows, so lines take a `gap` (or `deep`) that sets the space between blocks separately from their width. Small gaps mean that carrying too much speed lands you on a later block instead of in the lava; rising and zigzag lines punish overspeed the most and are kept for the later levels. Lines were tuned so tolerance falls through each set: about 60% in Lift and Bhop down to 15 to 30% in Terminal and Skyline, with Spire (0 to 6%) still the hardest.

Touch: on phones and tablets (a coarse primary pointer with touch, never in the desktop app) the page adds on-screen controls: a move stick that appears under the left thumb, drag anywhere on the right to look, a Jump button you hold to bunnyhop and drag to steer, a Crouch toggle, and CP, Reset and pause buttons. Auto strafe (on by default for touch) presses A or D in the air to match the way you swipe, since strafing needs both hands on a PC. Touch also starts at 70% render scale with shadows off, asks for fullscreen landscape, shrinks the menus on short screens and rewords the tutorial for the buttons. All of it lives in `buildTouch` in `src/game.js` behind `TOUCH`; mouse and keyboard play is untouched. Add `?touch=1` to the URL to try it on a computer.

Controller: standard gamepads work in the browser and desktop builds. Left stick moves, right stick looks (soft response curve), A or LB jumps, B, LT or the right stick click crouches, X goes back to the checkpoint, View restarts and Menu pauses; the D-pad, A and B drive the menus. The pad only takes over once it is used, so keyboard and mouse play is unchanged with one plugged in. Settings has controller look speed, auto strafe and invert. `pollPad` in `src/game.js`.

Level editor: Level editor on the title menu (keyboard and mouse). Fly with WASD, pick one of 22 pieces with 1 to 0, Shift+1 to 9 and Alt+1 to 3 (or step through them with - and =): block, floor, surf ramp, wall, start, checkpoint, finish, slope (a walkable wedge), half ramp (one surf face with a straight back), curved ramp (bends up to 180° left or right on a radius, with its own drop; the inside face runs each chord 40 units into the next like the Night City curves, the outside face meets them exactly so neither snags), bhop line (a row of blocks with a set gap), stairs, pillar, runner wall (red, for wallruns), tunnel, beam, lava (kills on touch) launch pad (throws you along its facing at a set speed and angle; at 0° it is a speed strip) and zipline (two red posts and a cable from the near post's top down to the far one; R/F sets the cable height, [ ] the drop, the wheel the length), and three moving pieces: moving platform (slides its distance ahead along its length and back), lift (rises and comes back down) and gate (a wall that rises to let you under, then drops); R/F sets how far they go, [ ] how fast (a gate's height is R/F, its rise [ ] and its speed , .), and the placement preview shows both ends of the trip. Click to place, right click to delete, middle click to copy a piece's settings, Q/E rotate 15°, wheel and Shift+wheel size, and R/F, [ ] and , . change the piece's other settings (the HUD says which, per piece: `ED` in `src/game.js`), T/G build height, Z undo, Enter test-plays from the start pad. Lava and launch pads act on what you stand on (`userHazards`), not on a trigger box, so rotated ones work. New piece types only ever go on the end of `ED_TYPES` (codes store the index); keep `TYPES` in `server/src/community.js` in step. Esc opens the panel: test, save to My levels (localStorage `strafe.levels.v1`), and share codes (`S1` + deflated, base64url JSON) that anyone can paste with Load a code. Custom levels are built far off the main map at x 600000, get a kill floor under their lowest piece, keep a local best and ghost, and never post to the online board.

Community levels: Publish in the editor panel puts the level on the Strafe server for everyone. It needs a level name, a Start and a Finish pad, and your best test-play run of the current layout, which the server checks like any board run and posts as the level's first time (so every published level can be beaten). The same layout can only be published once. Community on the title menu browses them by Top rated (thumbs up, then plays), Newest, Most played or Hardest (fewest finishes per play, levels with at least 3 plays first, shown as a finish rate), searches names and makers, and lists your own under Mine. Follow on a level saves its maker (localStorage `strafe.follow.v1`), Following lists levels by everyone you follow, and More by this maker lists one maker's levels (`GET /api/levels?by=<id>,<id>`, up to 100 ids; `sort=top|new|played|hard`). Each level shows its maker, plays, top times (race any ghost), a thumbs up, Open in editor and its level code; makers can take their own levels down. Community levels play as course `c:<id>` with their own online board, local best and ghost; they don't count toward stats, story or the level list. Server side: `server/src/community.js` reads share codes (keep it in step with edDecode and edFaces in game.js), with endpoints `GET /api/levels`, `GET /api/level`, `POST /api/levels/publish`, `/api/levels/like` and `/api/levels/delete`.

Share links: links to the game page on the Strafe server open straight into something. `?level=<course key>` offers that level, `?c=<id>` opens a community level, `?race=<code>` opens Online and joins that race room (once you have a name), and `?challenge=daily` or `?challenge=weekly` opens Challenges. A level or community link with `&vs=<player id>` loads that player's ghost and offers "<name> challenges you" with a Race their ghost button (plus Tutorial first for new players). The level complete card's Challenge a friend, the race room's Copy invite link and a community level's Copy link make them (the phone share sheet on touch, the clipboard elsewhere); the link is read once and taken off the address bar (`inviteOpen` in `src/game.js`). The server runs first for `/` (`run_worker_first` in `server/wrangler.toml`) and rewrites the page's preview tags for these links from `GET /api/card`, so a pasted link shows, for example, "Maya challenges you: Lift / Beat 0:23.46". The preview image is `server/static/og.jpg`, copied next to the page by the deploy workflow.

Online: one leaderboard and live races for the browser, desktop and phone versions, served by `server/` (a Cloudflare Worker with two Durable Objects: Board keeps players, best times and ghosts in SQLite; Race is one room per code, over websockets). It runs at https://strafe-online.strafe-game.workers.dev, which also hosts the game itself. Pushing to main deploys it through `.github/workflows/server.yml` using the repo secrets CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN and CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID, then runs `server/test/api.js` against the live server. Players pick a name the first time they post (the key for it stays in localStorage `strafe.online.v1`); their earlier bests come over once, with ghosts where the device has them and as older times where it doesn't. Every run goes up with its ghost and is checked (`server/src/check.js`): it starts on the start pad, ends in the finish, its sample count matches its time, and it never moves more than the 3500 u/s cap allows except when respawning at a checkpoint. After changing a level run `node server/gen-levels.cjs`; `node -r ./server/test/record.cjs test/routes.js` runs a bot suite through the same checks. Races: Online, then Make a room and share the 5 letter code; the host picks the level and starts, everyone loads in held on the start pad for a 4 second countdown, positions go out ten times a second and show as live runners with name tags, and the room times each finish from the go. Without the server (for example if the claude.ai page can't reach it) the game falls back to the claude.ai board.

Replay viewer: watch any run that has a ghost, from Watch best run on the level select, Watch best (V) on the finish screen, or Watch on any board row in the Online pane. Play or pause (Space), skip a second (arrows, Shift for five), step one sample (, and .), speed 0.1x to 4x (- and +), scrub with the bar, and switch camera with C: follow (drag to swing round, wheel to zoom), first person, or a free camera (WASD, Q and E for down and up, Shift for fast; keyboard and mouse only, hidden on phones). Controller: A play, B exit, LB and RB speed, D-pad skip, Y camera, right stick look, left stick flies the free camera. Replays only move the ghost runner and the camera; no physics runs.

Saved replays: the finish screen's Save replay (S) keeps that run. Replays on the title menu lists them, newest first, with a level filter, Watch and Delete, plus your personal best on each level. Up to 60 are kept in localStorage (`strafe.replays.v1` for the list, `strafe.replay.v1.<id>` for each run); runs saved before a level was rebuilt are marked as an older layout. Custom editor levels are not saved.

The manual 180 quick turn (Q, controller Y, the phone's 180 button) is gone. The automatic turn when you kick off a wall climb stays.

Medals: every level has bronze, silver, gold and platinum times. The ten story levels use their star targets for gold and silver; the Gauntlet and Night City scale from the bot times in `MEDAL_PAR` (gold 0.9x, silver 1.3x; platinum is 0.88x gold and bronze 1.5x silver), so re-time a level with its bot test when it is rebuilt. A community level's platinum is its maker's best time (shown as Author), with gold, silver and bronze at 1.15x, 1.4x and 2x; the server sends it as `authorTicks`. Medals come from your local best: the level complete card shows the one this run earned and the next to chase, level select shows your medal and all four times, the level cards carry a medal dot, the community page lists the times, and Stats counts platinums and golds and adds a medal column.

Challenges (title menu) has Daily and Weekly tabs. Daily: one level a day for everyone, picked by the server from the built-in levels and the top 10 community levels with at least 2 likes, by a hash of the UTC date (never the same as yesterday), then fixed for the day in the `daily` table. Every non-practice run finished on that level that day also goes on the day's board (`d:<YYYY-MM-DD>`, with ghosts to race), and the level complete card shows the daily rank. The pane shows the level, your time and rank, the board, time until the next level, and your streak (days in a row finished, kept in `strafe.daily.v1`). Endpoint `GET /api/daily`; `/api/run` answers with `daily` when the run counted. `wrangler dev --var DAILY_FORCE:<level key>` pins the daily level for local tests. Weekly: one level a week (Monday to Sunday UTC) picked the same way, never last week's level or today's daily, kept in the `weekly` table with its own board `w:<Monday date>` and a streak of weeks in a row (`GET /api/weekly`; `/api/run` answers with `weekly`).

Endless (title menu): a course with no finish line. `endless(seed)` in `src/map.js` builds it a section at a time with the route kit (`routeBuilder`, the resumable form of `routeKit`): each section is two or three pieces (block line, surf run with transfers, zipline, wallrun gap, curved ramp), each ending on a pad, and its last pad is a gate (checkpoint `idx` = sections cleared). Pieces get harder by tier (every three sections, up to Harbor-hard at tier 5). Turns and bends steer it back toward a centre far from the other levels, and updraft pads (`t: 'updraft'`, `props.updraft` handled in `physics.js`: the same throw up and forward every time) climb it back up whenever it has dropped below `ENDLESS.low`. A city of scenery towers sits far below each section. The game (`enStart` in `src/game.js`) keeps the section behind you and two ahead, building the next and dropping the old one at each gate. One life: a fall ends the run on the level complete card. Today's course comes from a hash of the UTC date, so it is the same for everyone, and posts sections and time to the day's board (`GET /api/endless`, `POST /api/endless` with `{ day, sections, ticks }`, ranked by most sections then time; the server can only check the numbers are possible). Random course uses a random seed and posts nothing. Local bests are in `strafe.endless.v1`. Bot test: `node test/endless.js [first seed] [seeds] [sections]` rides every section of each seed with only its neighbours built, as in the game.

No checkpoints (Settings, Movement): checkpoints still count for splits and the next checkpoint marker still points the way, but a fall (or F) sends you back to the start pad, which restarts the run. Practice mode ignores it. Finishing a level of two or more stages this way counts toward the No safety net achievement.

Fall map: on community levels the game notes where runners fall off (where they were 1.5 s before the fall registered, about where they left the course; lava where it caught them) and where they give up (restart or leave a run more than 3 s in, not just after a respawn), and posts them in batches (`POST /api/falls`, counted per 128-unit cell in the `falls` table). A maker's own level has a Fall map button in Community that plays it with the map drawn on top, and the editor panel has a Fall map button for a level that came from Community or was published from the editor (`ed.level.pub`). Falls show as balls, yellow through red where most fell, give-ups in purple, sized by count and drawn through walls (`GET /api/falls?id=`).
Moving pieces: `world.addMover(brushes, travel, time)` in `src/physics.js` keeps a mover's brushes where they were built and gives them an offset (`brush.off`) that every trace applies; `world.setTime(t)` eases each one out along `travel` and back, `time` seconds each way, and keeps the step since the last call. The game sets the clock each tick from when the level loaded, so a restart puts every mover back. Standing on one carries you (up and down fully, sideways unless a wall stops you), and one that moves into you shoves you along. Meshes are separate per piece and drawn between ticks. `test/movers.js` covers riding, lifts, shoving and gates. Desktop 1.0.17 and older can't open levels with them.

Set runs: on a Gauntlet, Night City, Harbor or Cableway level, Set run plays the whole set back to back on one clock (the levels' run times added up; the walk-in at each start isn't counted). R restarts the set from its first level, so each level is one unbroken attempt; finishing a level goes straight on to the next. Your best set (`strafe.sets.v1`) keeps every level's ghost (`strafe.setghost.v1.<set>`), which the next set run races, and splits show against it at each level. The Strafe server checks each level as a normal run and keeps a set board `set:<set>` (`POST /api/setrun`, set orders in `server/src/sets.json`, written by `gen-levels.cjs`); Online lists the set boards, where Race runs a set against someone's set ghost.

Ziplines: `world.ziplines` holds cables as `{ a, b }`, a the high end (`src/physics.js`, `tryZip`/`zipMove`, tested by `test/zipline.js`). Pass your hands (`zipHang`, 84 above your feet) within `zipReach` of a cable in the air and you grab it; you ride toward the low end at no less than 450 u/s, gaining speed from the slope up to 1600. Jump lets go with a hop, crouch drops you, and riding off the end (or into something) keeps the speed. The editor's zipline piece and routeKit's `zip` segment make cables; falling off is not checked while you ride, since a cable can cross a long drop.

Cableway: a fifth set of four levels (Wireline, Cable Car, Highwire, Long Haul; keys S1 to S4) built around ziplines by `routeKit`'s `zip` segment: a cable from a high pad down to the next piece, red posts beside it. Zips drop into pads, bhop lines and straight onto surf ramps (`skylineOrder` in map.js). `test/routes.js` rides them.

Level pass (2026-10-15): `routeKit` gained Mirror's Edge pieces and moving ones, and the levels use them so wallruns and finales stop repeating. `w2w`: two or three billboards down a narrow alley, swapping sides and overlapping, run one and kick across to the next (exit onto a pad 250 to 300 lower: each kick loses height). A `wall` can now come straight off the last block of a line (no pad before it) or lead straight into a block line, a ramp or a cable (`zip` with `sx`/`hang`: the cable starts beside the wall's end, so you run off the wall onto it). Pads take `climb: true` with `rise` (the front is a wall up to 190 high: jump into it, climb, pull up), `vault: [at]` (knee rails), `slot: [at]` (a wall with a gap under it to slide through) and `gate: [{ at, up, time }]` (a door that rises and drops on the level clock). Block lines take `sway: { d, time, from }`: blocks slide side to side, neighbours opposite ways. A `zip` right after another `zip` hangs `fall` below and `gap` past its end, so you drop from cable to cable with no roof. Moving pieces are `world.addMover` brushes driven by the same clock as editor pieces (`game.moverTick`); game.js draws each as its own mesh (`levelMoving`). The four finales each have one idea: Terminal (Gauntlet 6, now on `routeKit`, so `test/routes.js` rides it and `test/gauntlet.js` skips it) has departure gates on its pads, Skyline (Night City 6) is built on walls, Riptide (Harbor 6) has swinging blocks and two lock gates, and Long Haul (Cableway 4) is eight cables over three roofs. The bot (`test/routes.js`) knows every piece: it times gates, aims for where a swinging block will be when it lands, and strafes in for wallruns.

Speed tech (`src/physics.js`, tested by `test/speedtech.js`): a ramp slide keeps you airborne on a walkable slope while your speed along it is over `rampSlideSpeed` (1000), so you keep surfing instead of landing with friction (HUD: Rampslide). An edge bug happens when you fall faster than `edgeBugFall` (450) onto the very edge of a block with some sideways speed: your fall is cancelled and you carry on without landing, with a toast. Both count in Stats and have an achievement.

Achievements: 34 goals in `ACH` in `src/game.js` (speed, move counts, medals, finishing whole sets, a flawless run, community, races, the daily streak). They are checked every half second of play and on each finish; ones your saved bests and stats already meet are granted quietly at startup. A banner slides in when you earn one, Stats lists them all, and twelve cosmetics are unlocked by achievement count instead of stars (`ach` on the item: skins Neon 5, Circuit 10, Aurora 15, Sunset 25, Volt 30, Royal 35; trails Ion 5, Sakura 15, Comet 20, Plasma 25, Inferno 30, Borealis 35). Kept in localStorage (`strafe.ach.v1`).

Stats (title menu): time played, jumps, top speed, distance, runs started and finished, personal bests set, falls, wallruns, wall jumps, slides, vaults, levels beaten and story stars, then a table of every level with its best time, finishes, tries and time played. Counters live in localStorage (`strafe.stats.v1`) and start the first time this version runs; best times come from the saved bests. Demo, replays and the menu don't count, and practice runs don't set top speed or count as runs.

Key rebinding (Controls, keyboard and mouse only): each action (move, jump, crouch, restart, checkpoint, practice mode, practice save and load, tutorial, mute) has one key you can change, including middle, right, back and forward mouse buttons. Taking a key that another action uses swaps them. The arrows, right Shift and C stay as extra keys until another action takes them, and the wheel always jumps. Saved in `strafe.keys.v1`; with the defaults nothing changes. The Controls table, the HUD keys, the help line and the keyboard tutorial show your keys.

Accessibility (Settings): colour modes (standard, red and green friendly, blue and yellow friendly, high contrast) recolour the route pieces through the paint material's shader and the ahead and behind colours on splits and the ghost gap; camera tilt scales wallrun and landing roll; switches for the landing roll flip and screen flashes; HUD size; and Reduce motion, which turns off tilt, bob, FOV kick, landing kick, speed lines, flips and flashes in one press.

HUD helpers: a next checkpoint marker (a red diamond on the next checkpoint, white for the finish, with the distance in metres) shows where the route goes; when the checkpoint is off screen or behind you it rides the screen edge with an arrow, and it steps above the speed readout instead of covering it. Settings has a switch for it and a crosshair style (dot, cross, circle or none). Falling off respawns you once you are 700 units below the course pieces around you (`fallGrid()` in map.js; off the side of a course, below the last pieces you were near), or as soon as you land on scenery such as a street or a skyline roof, on every level including player-made ones, on top of the kill volumes under each level. Respawning after a fall or with F dips the screen to black for a moment (off with Screen flashes), and the level select opens on the last level you played (`strafe.last.v1`).

Polish: speed lines streak past the screen edges above about 450 u/s, hard landings nod and jolt the camera, footsteps and landings sound different on concrete, metal, wooden blocks, tiled pads and gravel (`SURFACE` in `src/game.js`, `step` in `src/audio.js`), and Settings has speed lines and landing kick toggles plus a Performance group (render scale 50 to 100%, frame rate counter).

Wallrun sound: catching a wall plays a shoe squeak, palm slap and thump, and the run itself is a scuffing loop that rises in pitch as the wallrun goes on. Mantle boost: a mantle you come into below 150 u/s (speed just before hitting the ledge) pops you out at `mantleExitMin` (280), easing back to the old 200 floor by 250 u/s; faster mantles are unchanged.

Parkour grace (`parkourGrace`, 0.12 s, in `src/physics.js`, tested by `test/grace.js`): a jump pressed just after you run or slide off a ledge still jumps (not when something is right under you, so stepping onto a surf ramp doesn't count), one pressed just after a wallrun or a climb ends still kicks off that wall, and one pressed in the first moment of a climb, before a kick is allowed, is kept and kicks as soon as it is. Speeds, air strafing and surfing are unchanged.

Strafe tips (Settings, on by default, keyboard and mouse only): after a weak air strafe (under 55% on the strafe meter for most of it, while holding A or D), a line under the meter says what held it back most: W held in the air, the mouse turning against the key, turning too slowly, or turning faster than your speed. At most one tip every 5 seconds; the keys it names follow your bindings.

Default tuning matches CS:GO surf/KZ servers: gravity 800, maxspeed 250, accelerate 5.5,
airaccelerate 150, friction 5.2, stopspeed 80, jump height 57. All of it lives in `DEFAULTS` in `src/physics.js`.

Ghost replay: every run is sampled every 4 ticks; a new best (or the first finish on a level with no ghost yet) is stored in localStorage (`strafe.ghost.v1.<level>`) and replays as a see-through runner with a marker that shows through walls, plus a live "Ghost +/-s" gap under the timer. Toggle it in Settings. `__strafe.step` and `__strafe.ghost` are exposed for browser tests.

Story mode: the Story button in the menu opens a courier campaign (a prologue on the tutorial plus ten jobs, one per level) with briefings, debriefs and up to three stars per job for beating time targets. Jobs unlock in order; progress is in localStorage (`strafe.story.v1`). When the five route levels were rebuilt, local bests, splits and ghosts for them were cleared once (`strafe.rebuild.v2`); when the surf in Surf, Canyon, Crossover and Cascade was made harder they were cleared again (`strafe.rebuild.v3`, and Cascade again in `strafe.rebuild.v4` to `v6` as it was reworked). After the difficulty pass on the bhop lines, Bhop, Stairway, Crossover and every Gauntlet and Night City level were cleared once more (`strafe.rebuild.v7`). The jobs and targets are `STORY` in `src/game.js`.

Cosmetics: story stars and achievements unlock 16 runner skins (they recolour the first-person arms, legs and the ghost; `glow` makes the accent emissive, and Prism, Aurora and Sunset cycle their accent colour) and 15 trails (a camera-facing ribbon behind you and your ghost above 300 u/s, drawn in one of seven shader styles: ribbon, glow, pulse, spark, twin, fire or glitch, `TRAIL_STYLES`; `add` trails blend additively and `scale` widens them). Pick them from Cosmetics on the title menu, where a small runner jogs a turntable wearing whatever you point at (locked items too; tap a locked one to keep it in the preview); the choice is saved in settings. They are `SKINS` and `TRAILS` in `src/game.js`.

Models: the low city far below the levels is KayKit City Builder buildings (CC0), water towers stand on the skyline roofs, and cars, street lights and street clutter sit on the open streets. The ghost and the Cosmetics preview are an animated runner model (X Bot, idle and run). In first person the same model is your body: the old block rig still runs hidden, and the model's arm, leg and finger bones copy its limb directions every frame, so every move keeps its pose; the head is shrunk away so the camera sits inside it. `build.js` inlines `vendor/` (three.js r128 `GLTFLoader` and `SkeletonUtils`) and every `assets/*.glb` as base64, so the page is still one file (about 1.8 MB). The models are instanced in square cells that are only drawn in view and in range; far low-city cells swap back to plain blocks. None of it collides, so physics and the bot tests are unchanged. Sources and licences are in `assets/README.md`.

Online leaderboard: inside claude.ai the published game uses the artifact `db` and `user` capabilities. Each player's best per level is one shared document, `times/<their id>`, that only they can write (rules: `times` read view / write owner, `times/{self}` write interact); level select shows the top ten per level with names from the viewer's claude.ai profile, and the finish screen shows your online rank. Reworked levels post under new fields (`REBUILT` in `src/game.js`: `bhop_v2`, `surf_v3`, `cascade_v6` and so on) so old times drop off. Only runs at the standard air acceleration (150) are posted, local bests are uploaded once, and outside claude.ai the board is hidden.
